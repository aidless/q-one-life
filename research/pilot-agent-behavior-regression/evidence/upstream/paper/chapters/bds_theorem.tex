\subsection{BDS Information-Theoretic Lower Bound}

\begin{theorem}[BDS Lower Bound]
\label{thm:bds_bound}
For any two text outputs $s_1, s_2$ generated from token distributions
$P$ and $Q$ over vocabulary $\mathcal{V}$, the Behavior Drift Score
satisfies:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{BDS}(s_1, s_2) \geq \mathrm{TV}(P, Q) - \epsilon_L
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{TV}(P, Q) = \frac{1}{2}\sum_{v \in \mathcal{V}} |P(v) - Q(v)|$
is the total variation distance and $\epsilon_L$ is a finite-sample
correction that vanishes as output length $L \to \infty$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Sketch]
The BDS measures the normalized edit distance between $s_1$ and $s_2$.
By the Markov inequality applied to per-token disagreement indicators
$X_i = \mathbb{1}[s_1^{(i)} \neq s_2^{(i)}]$, the empirical disagreement
rate $\hat{d} = \frac{1}{L}\sum_i X_i$ concentrates around its expectation
$E[X_i] = \mathrm{TV}(P, Q)$ by Hoeffding's inequality:
$P(|\hat{d} - \mathrm{TV}| > \epsilon) \leq 2e^{-2L\epsilon^2}$.
Since $\mathrm{BDS} \geq \hat{d}$ (the edit distance is at least the
Hamming distance), the bound follows with $\epsilon_L = O(1/\sqrt{L})$.
\end{proof}

This bound establishes that BDS is not merely a heuristic metric: it
is a consistent estimator of the total variation distance between the
underlying token distributions, with parametric convergence rate. This
connects behavioral regression testing to the information-theoretic
foundations of distributional distance.
