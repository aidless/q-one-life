
\noindent When a benchmark's labels come from language models, the label producers could compete in the ranking they defined. We treat a gold generator's leave-one-out score as a \emph{noise floor}: under a plurality convention it is the maximum over generators. The floor belongs to how the gold was defined, so measuring it reveals when consensus-gold constructions fail to yield verdicts. No public board measured here clears that rule: the contribution is the refusal semantics, the three rulers, and the measurements that force the refusal.

Three rulers are implemented (leave-one-out over model gold; a median-annotator substitution for human gold; a latent-variable Dawid--Skene arm that estimates a different quantity and feeds no verdict), separating refusals from graded readings: a corpus whose premises fail returns no verdict. Both public corpora have two generators, and under either tie rule their item sets are non-coextensive, so the aligning rule reduces the floor to an agreement rate. A 25-generator human-gold construction does return verdicts: three unreadable readings, each with the leader at its own floor, plus one domain refusal, and a released panel of 17 judge models returns the same zero margin. Where the ranked field is the pool that made the gold, the declared \mbox{vacate-on-tie} convention makes the plurality floor the leader's own score, so the margin has nothing to bind: the zero is an identity. Human gold leaves the floor undefined, and the latent arm measures no task.

A synthetic counterexample shows that clearing producer baseline is not a necessary condition for every resolvable top ordering: with three perfect producers setting floor=1.0, systems scoring 0.9 and 0.5 remain deterministically ordered yet the gate refuses. Therefore clearance of that gate cannot by itself be read as a general ranking noise bound. No public board measured here fires the READABLE arm outside the test suite's positive control; calibration on a released panel certifies only its own debiased alignment. A preregistered disjoint-field deployment on a second release remains pending as C04. We release the tool, counterexample witness, and test suite.
